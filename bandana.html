Bandanas — Complete Vendor Breakdown

Every way to brand a bandana — ranked for a full-bleed cotton result, with our pick at the top.

The three ways to do it

You have three routes. The cheapest/most traditional is a stock paisley with a blank center you fill with the seal (A); the most hands-on is buying blanks and branding them yourself (B); and the one you want — a genuinely bespoke product — is a completely custom, edge-to-edge design that turns the whole bandana into Poco Loco paisley + Western iconography (C). Below: the honest method trade-offs, our vetted pick, and a wide ranked vendor list.

Approach A

Open-center medallion

A stock traditional paisley with a blank center medallion; you or the vendor screen the seal into the open middle. Real cotton, classic, cheap, low-ish MOQ — but the paisley is generic and the logo is confined to the center.

Approach B

Blank → brand in-house

Buy blank earthy-cotton paisley or solid bandanas and brand them yourself (UVDTF / screen / embroidered corner). Total control, any quantity — but in-house transfer can't achieve true edge-to-edge all-over, and washes less durably than dyed-in print.

Approach C — your goal

Full-custom, all-over

Your complete design — custom paisley + the Poco Loco seal + iconography — printed edge to edge. The premium, fully-branded result. On cotton via all-over screen or digital print; the easy/cheap path is dye-sublimation but that's polyester; the authentic look is rotary screen on USA cotton (high MOQ).

Full-bleed on cotton — DTLA Print (and The Bandanna Co. for a flagship run)

DTLA Print (Vernon, CA) · The Bandanna Company (USA mill, pictured)

For your full-bleed, all-over cotton goal at a realistic ~24–300 units, DTLA Print is the best practical pick: all-over screen print on 100% cotton, hemmed on all four sides, edge-to-edge, US-made (Vernon, CA), effective MOQ ~24. A classic paisley field + your 1–3 color seal as a centered medallion is exactly its sweet spot.

  • Lowest MOQ US cotton full-bleed: Printology (full-bleed screen on cotton, MOQ 12).
  • Full-color digital on USA cotton, verified price: 4AllPromos ($11.76/ea at 100) or Planet Apparel (Austin, TX; organic option).
  • Authentic heirloom flagship: The Bandanna Company / Hav-A-Hank (pictured) — genuine USA-mill rotary all-over cotton paisley, the real thing — but the all-over rotary look needs ~3,000 pcs.
  • Easy & cheap (poly): Bandana Supply (local Sugar Land, TX), Pop! Promos — gorgeous full-bleed via dye-sublimation, but polyester, which reads athletic, not rustic.

Methods, art & finishing

Cotton vs. polyester — the core decisionDye-sublimation gives effortless edge-to-edge full color but only on polyester (reads athletic/synthetic — wrong for an upscale-rustic ranch). A true all-over cotton bandana means all-over screen (finite spot colors — perfect for paisley + a 1–3 color seal), digital/pigment (unlimited color, back prints lighter), or reactive/rotary (deepest, most premium, but high MOQ). For Poco Loco, stay cotton — a classic paisley + your black-and-white seal is ideal for screen.Art-setup gotchas for full-bleed
  • Bleed: extend the paisley ~0.25″ past the cut/hem line so no white slivers show after hemming.
  • Safe area: a hem folds ~0.25–0.5″ under each side — keep the seal & text ≥1.5–2″ from the raw edge.
  • Layout: build the paisley as a seamless tile; seal as a centered medallion with a decorative inner border (the classic bandana look).
  • Color: specify Pantone Solid Coated; exact match is guaranteed only on white/natural grounds — design on a natural base, not a pre-dyed color.
  • Sides: sublimation/reactive print through to the back; screen/digital usually print one side (natural-cotton back) — the most cost-effective premium layout.
